# AI Customer-Conversation Course Plan

Delivery and authoring plan: eight live sessions, ten separately shareable topic documents, and practical customer-facing exercises. Revised to incorporate the timing, scope, and document-design review.

Source: 10-Day_AI_Customer-Conversation_Training_Program (1).docx. The source supplies ten topics and the customer-facing learning objectives. The eight-session grouping, timings, document briefs, and completion criteria below are proposed course-design decisions.

## Course structure

- **Audience:** Customer-facing employees learning to explain AI, identify useful business applications, and discuss approved Talent Genie capabilities accurately.
- **Level:** Beginner; no coding required.
- **Live delivery:** Eight sessions of 45 minutes each on Google Meet, totaling six hours.
- **Time budget:** Each session contains 40 minutes of planned activity and five minutes of contingency. Across the course: 320 planned minutes plus 40 buffer minutes. Group setup and transitions are included in the planned activity, not charged to the buffer.
- **Topic documents:** Ten separate handouts, one for each original topic. Each merged session has two distinct handouts.
- **Learning website:** A small course website is the learner hub. It presents the eight-session path, provides each of the ten topic documents, and includes one short quiz for each topic.
- **Cadence:** Suggested delivery over two weeks; exact dates remain open. Topic numbers identify the source topics, not calendar days.
- **Learning approach:** Essential explanations, relevant examples, practical activities, and feedback.
- **Learner outputs:** Customer explanations, a prompt, verification notes, data-use decisions, a pilot brief, and discovery notes.

Combine Topics 1 and 2 because shared examples can explain both what AI does and which capabilities fit which tasks. Combine Topics 5 and 8 because selecting a use case naturally leads to choosing a specific step in a workflow.

Keep LLM behavior, prompting, accuracy, data protection, discovery, and product conversations as separate sessions. Each needs practice and feedback. Move the combined use-case/workflow session after accuracy and data protection so learners apply those checks to their proposals.

All required learner work should fit inside the sessions. Publish documents on the course website before the relevant session and retain them as practical references afterward. Do not depend on advance reading to make a session work. Learners join live teaching through Google Meet. Label additional exercises as optional extensions outside the six live hours.

The topic quizzes are short reinforcement checks, normally three to five questions with immediate answer feedback. Decide during website setup whether results are private self-checks or named completion records. Do not collect learner identity or results until the approved tracking method and privacy expectations are confirmed.

Each six-minute round includes 90 seconds to confirm the supplied customer problem, 90 seconds of explanation, one minute for an objection and next step, and two minutes of observer feedback. Rotate employee, customer, and observer roles so each learner practices as the employee. Preassign groups before the session. For a remainder of two, use a pair with facilitator observation; for a remainder of one, add a facilitator or coach to make a workable group. Confirm staffing before delivery rather than squeezing four learner turns into three rounds.

Learners adapt three short, partly completed templates for a recruiter, operations leader, and executive, and deliver one in the role-play. Session 7 provides longer discovery practice; this final exercise checks problem confirmation, not a complete new discovery interview. This session is an applied learning check. Formal individual certification, if required, needs assessor coverage or separately scheduled assessment time; peer feedback alone does not establish readiness for customer work.

## Standard for the ten topic documents

Create ten individually shareable documents. Each should support the live activity and help an employee perform the same task later without a facilitator.

Create one website quiz for each topic document. The quiz should test the document's observable outcome, use realistic customer or work decisions, explain the correct answer, and identify safety-critical questions where applicable. A merged live session therefore has two separate topic quizzes even though learners use one working document during the call.

**Teaching-text target:** Approximately 600–900 words, usually two to three readable pages, for the essential explanation, short worked example, and customer language. This is not a total-document word or page limit.

**Exercise and reference appendices:** Keep worksheets, longer scenarios, reference extracts, full policy tables, three self-check questions, and answer rationales outside the teaching-text word count. Label these sections clearly within the same shareable topic document. Put the activity and blank working sheet before the model answer; identify the exact working page in the session agenda. Document 06 needs its fictional brief and evidence extracts; Document 07 needs complete policy context for every scenario. Do not shorten those materials merely to meet a word target.

Use the same practical structure for every document:

1. **Customer or work question:** The real problem this topic helps the learner handle.
2. **Observable outcome:** One clear statement beginning “After this topic, you can…”
3. **Essential explanation:** Three to five central ideas in plain language.
4. **Worked example:** Relevant input, response or decision, and an explanation of why it works.
5. **Customer language:** A short explanation to use and a common claim to avoid.
6. **Reusable aid:** A topic-specific template, checklist, decision guide, or question set.
7. **Core exercise and three self-check questions:** Include a brief model response or rationale after the exercise. Use these questions as the starting point for the website quiz, then rewrite them as needed for clear feedback. Keep facilitator scoring notes separate.
8. **References and maintenance:** Source material and, where relevant, approved product or policy references, review date, and content owner.

Before sharing, check that:

- Every section helps the learner explain something to a customer, perform a relevant task, or make an appropriate decision.
- Examples connect to customer conversations, recruiting administration, sales preparation, support responses, or operations.
- Technical detail explains a practical behavior or limitation. Mathematical detail and model architecture are outside this beginner course.
- Examples identify the input, useful result, and required human review.
- Each document has a distinct purpose. Cross-reference related handouts instead of repeating whole sections.
- Fictional examples are labeled; generic AI uses are not described as Talent Genie features; proposed benefits are not presented as measured results.
- The reusable aid is easy to find during work.

## Topic-document briefs

### 01 — Explaining AI in plain language

**Customer question:** “What could AI actually help our team do?”

Cover a simple definition, drafting, summarizing, classification, pattern recognition, human judgment, and common misconceptions. Use a fictional customer-conversation summary to show usefulness and a limitation.

**Reusable aid:** A 60-second explanation framework: what AI does → customer example → limitation → review needed.

**Live exercise:** Prepare and deliver the explanation using the prompt reproduced on Document 02's working sheet. It must be understandable, relevant, and avoid implying that AI always knows the correct answer. Document 01 remains the standalone explanation and read-after reference.

**Boundary:** Explain usefulness and limitations here; reserve capability comparisons for Document 02.

### 02 — Matching AI capabilities to business needs

**Customer question:** “Do we need a chatbot, forecasting, generated content, or ordinary automation?”

Teach three practical uses live: find patterns, generate content, and automate steps. These are a teaching framework, not mutually exclusive AI categories. The reference comparison table retains machine learning, generative AI, conversational AI, predictive AI, and workflow automation, explains their overlap, and notes that automation can work without AI. Point briefly to predictive uses and conversational interfaces without teaching a five-part taxonomy live.

**Reusable aid:** A comparison table connecting business task, suitable approach, input, and review point. Its working sheet contains the three core scenarios and the 60-second explanation prompt, so learners need only this document open in Session 1.

**Live exercise:** Match three short scenarios to an approach and explain one choice to a partner. Include one case where a simple rule or existing process is sufficient. Add two optional scenarios.

**Boundary:** Teach selection logic instead of a changing catalog of vendors.

### 03 — Understanding AI answers and their limitations

**Customer question:** “Why can the AI give a polished answer that is wrong?”

Explain learned language patterns, tokens, context limits, and generated responses in plain language. Distinguish generating an answer from verifying it. Use prepared examples with missing context and an unsupported assertion.

**Reusable aid:** A customer FAQ about incorrect answers, missing context, and checking important claims.

**Live exercise:** Give a two-minute explanation without “algorithm,” “neural network,” or “probability model.”

**Boundary:** Explain why review is necessary; Document 06 supplies the process. Check any product-specific behavior against current authoritative documentation during authoring.

### 04 — Writing prompts for useful business results

**Work question:** “How do I turn a vague request into a useful draft?”

Cover goal, context, instructions, constraints, audience, format, examples, and revision. Show weak and improved prompts for a fictional customer follow-up email.

**Reusable aid:** A fill-in prompt template that requires missing information to be flagged and unsupported claims to be avoided.

**Live exercise:** Improve a prompt, inspect its output, and revise one instruction. Supply prepared responses if tool access is unavailable.

**Success check:** Goal, context, constraints, and format are present; the revision addresses an actual weakness.

### 05 — Choosing useful AI applications at work

**Customer question:** “Where could AI help us in our day-to-day work?”

Cover selected examples in support, sales preparation, recruiting administration, document review, and operations. Distinguish assistance, recommendations, and automated actions. Name each example's input, potential value, limitation, and human owner.

**Reusable aid:** Use-case cards: problem → AI task → proposed benefit → review point.

**Live exercise:** Compare three supplied use cases and select one to take into Document 08's pilot template.

**Boundary:** This document helps choose a task; Document 08 explains how to test it within a workflow. Document 05 can be completed and shared with generic business examples. Only optional Talent Genie examples require the approved product brief. During Session 6, use the three cards reproduced in Document 08 and retain Document 05 as the read-after reference.

### 06 — Checking AI output before using it

**Work question:** “What needs checking before I share this answer with a customer?”

Organize the topic around the consequences of error: decide the verification effort first, then check the relevant claims. Cover hallucinations, names, dates, figures, quotations, citations, trusted current sources, and appropriate reviewers.

Use a fictional recruiting-account handover brief prepared for a customer call. Supply a short context card explaining the work and intended audience, so the example makes sense before the later business-use-case session. Include three planted material errors and enough reference extracts to check them within the exercise time.

**Proposed teaching ladder, subordinate to company policy:**

| Situation | Verification approach | Exercise example |
|---|---|---|
| Low-consequence, reversible internal drafting | Read for relevance and errors; confirm any factual statements before reuse | Internal brainstorming notes using fictional information |
| Customer-facing statements or operational commitments | Check material facts against trusted current records; resolve or remove unsupported claims before sharing | Candidate availability or a promised follow-up date |
| Output affecting hiring, legal, financial, privacy, safety, or major reputational outcomes | Obtain appropriate qualified human review and required approvals; stop if evidence or authority is insufficient | An unsupported recommendation to reject a candidate |

Classify by intended use and consequence, not by how confident the AI sounds or which tool produced it. A draft can move into a higher tier when its audience or use changes. This ladder is a training aid, not a substitute for company policy or specialist judgment.

**Reusable aid:** A checklist ordered as intended use → consequence of error → review level → claims and evidence → unresolved items → decision to use, revise, or escalate. The log records the claim, risk tier and reason, source or reviewer, finding, and status.

**Live exercise:** Assign a review level, find the errors, and identify unresolved claims. Then explain what changes if the brief is used for a hiring decision rather than an internal discussion. Supply an answer rationale tied to the evidence and proposed use.

**Success check:** Finds all three material errors and chooses a defensible verification level and reviewer. Asking the same model again is not treated as independent verification.

### 07 — Deciding what information can go into AI

**Work question:** “Can I use this customer information in this tool?”

Cover approved tools and data categories, minimum necessary information, identifying details, access and retention, output storage, incident reporting, bias, copyright, and human accountability. Do not assume every approved tool permits every data type.

**Live versus reference coverage:** Follow Session 5's explicit split. Keep permission decisions, bias recognition, human accountability, an incident response, and a brief content-reuse reminder in the live lesson. Put detailed access/retention tables, storage locations, licensing guidance, and full policy references in labeled appendices. Use one short biased candidate-summary example in the responsible-use demonstration and return to it during feedback.

**Reusable aid:** A policy-grounded decision guide and the actual company incident-reporting route.

**Live exercise:** Classify four contrasting data examples and handle one accidental-disclosure scenario. Include credentials, fictional information, and private candidate or customer records. Provide explicit tool and policy context for each example.

**Success check:** All designated critical decisions are correct. Removing names does not automatically make information safe to upload. Unclear permission requires checking the responsible policy or contact.

**Dependency:** Obtain actual company rules; do not invent approval categories or reporting contacts.

### 08 — Planning one useful AI workflow pilot

**Customer question:** “Where should we start, and how would we know it helped?”

Cover process steps, bottlenecks, task selection, inputs, permissions, integrations, review points, and success measures. Include a completed fictional example that keeps hiring decisions with accountable people.

**Reusable aid:** A one-page pilot brief: problem, selected step, proposed assistance, allowed input, reviewer, baseline, proposed target, owner, and stop condition.

**Live exercise:** Complete a partly filled brief using the supplied workflow and three use-case cards reproduced from Document 05. Apply the accuracy and data-use guides. Document 08 is the sole working document in Session 6.

**Scope:** Evaluate and improve a supplied workflow. Independent workflow mapping is not an assessed course outcome; offer it only as an explicitly separate extension.

**Success check:** Names a specific task, appropriate controls, and a measurable outcome. Fictional baseline figures and proposed targets are clearly labeled.

### 09 — Running a useful customer discovery conversation

**Work question:** “What should we understand before recommending AI?”

Cover current process, pain, frequency, impact, urgency, desired outcome, data readiness, decision-makers, timing, and constraints. Include a short annotated discovery exchange.

**Reusable aid:** A question guide and a one-sentence problem-statement template.

**Live exercise:** Within the 13-minute practice block, run two five-minute paired conversations, allow one minute to switch roles, then spend two minutes comparing notes. Pair setup and instructions have their own two-minute agenda block. Confirm the problem and desired outcome before discussing a solution.

**Success check:** Explores process and impact, acknowledges constraints, and checks understanding with the customer.

### 10 — Explaining Talent Genie and agreeing on the next step

**Customer question:** “How does Talent Genie help someone in my role, and what happens next?”

Cover approved capabilities, relevant outcomes, limitations, data and implementation needs, human oversight, and next steps. Provide examples for a recruiter, operations leader, and executive.

**Reusable aid:** Three adaptable talk tracks and an objection-response guide. Use customer problem → supported capability → relevant outcome → limitation or review need → next step.

**Live exercise:** Adapt the three templates and use one in the final role-play. Keep each explanation under 90 seconds.

**Success check:** Fits the audience, uses verified facts, avoids guarantees, and ends with a concrete action.

**Dependency:** The source names Talent Genie but does not describe its features. Obtain the approved product brief before completing or sharing this handout. Generic AI examples cannot establish product knowledge.

## Shared case study and facilitator materials

Use a fictional recruiting team that spends too much time preparing candidate updates and repeating administrative work. It wants faster turnaround, accurate communications, and human responsibility for hiring decisions.

Prepare synthetic messages, a process description, sample timing data, and customer priorities. Label all fictional details. Reveal an additional customer constraint during Session 7 so learners update their earlier assumptions. Include non-recruiting examples elsewhere to show how skills transfer.

The facilitator pack needs eight timed agendas, worked examples, answer keys, group instructions, and feedback criteria. Keep it separate from the ten learner documents. A combined workbook may be assembled later, but every topic must remain independently shareable.

## Learning website and Google Meet delivery

The website is a compact learning hub rather than a full learning-management system. Its first release should include:

- A course home page showing the eight sessions in order and the learner's next step.
- Eight session pages with the session outcome, topic links, downloadable documents, short preparation guidance, and the relevant Google Meet schedule or approved join-link location.
- Ten topic sections, each with its separately shareable document and a three-to-five-question quiz with answer explanations.
- Clear labels for the working document in Sessions 1 and 6.
- An instructor-controlled way to publish corrected document and quiz versions without leaving conflicting copies active.
- Responsive and keyboard-accessible pages, readable documents, and a tested fallback when a learner cannot access the site during a live session.

Before implementation, decide whether quizzes are self-check only or whether instructors need named completion records. If tracking is required, confirm the approved identity, storage, access, retention, and reporting approach before collecting results. A self-check-only first release can avoid accounts and persistent learner data.

Use Google Meet for all live sessions. Create eight calendar events, add the correct instructor and learners, include the session-page link, and verify screen sharing, captions, recording policy, breakout approach, and backup facilitation. Rehearse through the actual Google Meet setup for every session, not only the most complex sessions.

## Feedback and completion

Keep assessment proportionate to the six-hour course:

- Use the first opening question as a baseline and session exit checks to identify misconceptions.
- Provide one three-to-five-question website quiz for each of the ten topics, with immediate explanations. Use the approved tracking model: private self-check by default unless named result collection has been explicitly approved.
- Review the Session 3 prompt and Session 6 pilot brief as applied checkpoints.
- Use the final role-play to check discovery, clarity, accuracy, responsible use, and a relevant next step.
- Fit four short final knowledge questions into the allocated four minutes, including submission. Cover capability fit, verification level, safe data use, and an unsupported product claim. A proposed completion standard is three correct, with designated safety-critical items correct. Use earlier checkpoints for depth rather than extending the final session.
- Collect one main output per session. A merged session's output includes both topics without adding a second full assignment outside the schedule.
- Give corrective feedback and a targeted retry for unsafe recommendations or unsupported product claims.

For role-play feedback, score each of the five dimensions 0 (missing), 1 (partial), or 2 (clear). A suggested coaching target is eight out of ten, with no unresolved accuracy or responsible-use issue. Keep peer feedback separate from formal assessor decisions.

### Pilot and release criteria

- All ten documents have a clear outcome, relevant worked example, usable aid, exercise, self-checks, and answer rationale.
